# Technical Specifications & Design Decisions

## Overall Goals
- **Tow Vehicle:** Ford Ranger
- **Track Width and Bolt Pattern:** Matched exactly to the tow vehicle (6x139.7). Tire size: 265/60R18 Falken Wildpeak A/T3W on Ford original rims. The Ranger's underslung spare serves both car and trailer — no spare is carried on the trailer.
- **Manufacturing Technique:** Custom-built CNC router (PrintNC) is utilized maximally for precision, corner joints, brackets, niches, and modular panels. Additionally, 3D printed guides will be used to ensure precise drilling of holes in the steel beams. A simplified, non-CNC version with cruder geometry will also be provided for builders without access to CNC routing.

## 1. Chassis & Mechanics
- **Frame Structure:** Square steel (VKR 50x50x3 or 60x40x3 mm), frame 2000 x **1200 mm**. Crossbeams at ~500 mm spacing (x = 0 / 500 / 975 / 1500 / 2000): the mid beam takes the drawbar lap; the two floor crossbars are **OPTIONAL** (single toggle: `floor_crossbars` in `cad/main_assembly.scad`, mirrored as `FLOOR_CROSSBARS` in `scripts/calculate_mass.py` and `scripts/gen_frame_fea.py`) and exist for the **floor**, not the frame — they halve the formply's unsupported span under cargo point loads, provide lashing/body mounts, and the rear one is the water-tank hanger. Each end bolts to the rail through a **bottom T-plate** (same CNC part as the mid-crossbeam plates; no top plate — the bars are frame-neutral, so the joint only locates them and carries floor load in bolt shear, and the flush top face lets the formply screw straight onto the tube and close the joint from above). Cost of the pair: +16 kg incl. plates. (FEA: frame-member stresses are unchanged by them — rails are supported by the wheels and coupling, not by crossbars.) The frame was narrowed from 1400 mm because matching the Ranger's 1560 mm track is otherwise geometrically impossible: with 265 mm tires, tire-to-body clearance is (1560-265)/2 - 600 = **47.5 mm** at 1200 mm width, but negative at 1400 mm. No full welding to prevent fatigue cracks; the frame is bolted together with through-bolts (M10/M12 in 8.8/10.9 grade).
- **Bolted Joints (critical):** Every through-bolt runs through an **internal steel crush sleeve** (precision tube, e.g., 16x2.5 mm for M10) so the bolt can be torqued to full preload without collapsing the 3 mm RHS walls. Lock nuts and hardened washers throughout. **Re-torque all structural bolts after the first ~500 km, then annually.**
- **Drawbar:** Single central beam in **VKR 100x50x4 (standing on edge)**, chosen for clean looks over a V/A-frame — and sized for it: 100 kg tongue x 3g off-road dynamic factor x 1.09 m lever = 3.2 kNm; W = 28.8 cm3 gives ~111 MPa vs. S355 yield = safety factor ~3.2. (A 50x50x3 beam would see ~390 MPa — above yield — which is why the profile is upsized.) Lateral loads are handled by lapping the beam under the frame back to the **mid crossbeam** (x 950-1000 — the frame's ONE middle beam), so side moments resolve as a force couple over a ~0.95 m base. The beam ends at x=1020 — it must stop short of the torsion-axle tube, which crosses the centerline at the same depth (x 1070-1150). There is **no separate crossbeam over the axle**: the bolted axle tube itself ties the rails there via its brackets, so one middle beam serves both the drawbar lap and the frame. Attachment (DECIDED): at the **front crossbeam** the beam is CLAMPED by the angle-bracket pair (no holes in the flanges — see the joint bullet below); at the **mid crossbeam** it is through-bolted with M12 + crush sleeve through a 10 mm spacer plate (moment ~zero there; gives positive longitudinal location). All of this math is executable in `scripts/beam_check.py` (re-run after any geometry/mass change; CalculiX FEA setup in `fea/`, full pipeline via `scripts/run_fea.sh`). Note from the calculator: a *simultaneous* worst-case combination (2g vertical + 0.3g lateral, linearly superposed) gives SF ≈ 1.8 — acceptable given the stacked conservatisms, but VKR 120x60x4 (+2 kg) restores SF ≈ 2.7 if more margin is wanted.
- **Drawbar joint (the actual weak point):** The governing detail is not the beam but the **13 mm bolt hole through the tension flange at the front crossbeam** — net section −17%, kt ≈ 2.5, right at peak moment; fatigue margin only ~1.3× vs ~3-4× everywhere else (`check_joint_hole` in `beam_check.py`). Fix, keeping the 100x50x4 profile: clamp the beam at the front crossbeam instead of piercing its flanges — vertical bolts pass *beside* the beam, horizontal M12 bolts go through the beam webs at the **neutral axis** where bending stress is ~zero, and nothing references the beam's end cuts (maximally tolerance-forgiving). Three interchangeable implementations, same structural concept:
  1. **Angle-bracket pair (DECIDED — in the main assembly):** 2x hot-rolled **L80x80x8** S355, 120 mm long, straight cuts only, galvanized with the frame (`cad/drawbar_angle_joint.scad`). Leg-bending SF ≈ 3.4 with the pair coupled by the through-bolts (`check_angle_joint` in `beam_check.py`); 6 mm legs drop to SF 1.9 — use 8 mm. The 10 mm spacer plate stays.
  2. **CNC-milled 6082-T6 cradle** (`cad/drawbar_cradle.scad`): one-piece channel + wings, integrated spacer web — the premium/CNC variant.
  3. **Square U-bolts** (fyrkantbygel M12 for 50 mm profile) + top plate: fully off-the-shelf trailer hardware; add a saddle strip so the U-bend doesn't dent the tube corners. The V-drawbar alternative is preserved in `cad/drawbar_wedge_plate.scad` + git history. The coupling itself must be a type-approved unit with a documented rating.
- **Corner Reinforcements:** "Double sandwich" with heavy-duty, CNC-milled 8-10 mm aluminum plates (6082-T6) on the top and bottom of each 90-degree corner.
- **Surface Treatment:** The steel frame is hot-dip galvanized after drilling. Holes are drilled 11 mm (M10) / 13 mm (M12) and **reamed clean after galvanizing** — hot-dip adds ~100 µm of zinc per surface.
- **Corrosion Protection:** Duralac (anodic paste) on all steel–aluminum interfaces; optionally a thin *rigid* isolator sheet (0.5-1 mm G10/FR4). **No rubber or vinyl inside structural joints** — elastomers creep under sustained compression and destroy bolt preload, which is the exact loosening/fatigue failure mode the bolted design must avoid. Note that galvanized steel against aluminum is a relatively benign pairing (zinc is anodic to aluminum), so paste + galvanizing is sufficient protection.
- **Suspension:** Torsion (rubber) axles have limited travel (~70 mm) and no damping — on washboard/corrugated roads they overheat and pound the payload. Specify **shock absorbers (damper kit)** with the torsion axle as a minimum; evaluate axle-less trailing-arm setups (e.g., Timbren) if serious off-road use is intended.

## 2. Electrical System & Electronics (LEFT Side)
- **Placement (sides in TRAVEL direction):** All electrical is concentrated on the **right side**: the electrical bay (removable power-station box, IP65 cabinet, el-niche with router panel) sits mid-right, directly **ahead of the fridge drawer** (rear right). The 230V CEE inlet sits on the front wall's right half, mounted high — a short, clean feed down into the right-side bay. **NOTE:** the old strict gas-left/electric-right separation principle is *parked* while the interior layout settles (decision 2026-07-08: layout first, then refine gas routing/placement around it).
- **Cable Routing:** Cables in corrugated conduits inside the steel frame. The conduits exit the steel before the corners and are routed in a soft curve protected within the tunnel between the corner plates.
- **Grounding:** Single-Point Grounding (Star grounding). The steel frame is equipotentially bonded with a 6 mm² yellow/green cable to a grounding terminal in an IP65 electrical cabinet.
- **230V Network:** Blue CEE inlet -> Fuse box (RCD + 10A/13A circuit breakers) -> Outlets (fridge, Noco charger, IP66 external).
- **12V & Battery Module:** Detachable Power Station box with a **200 Ah LiFePO4** bank, DC-DC MPPT charger, Noco charger, and Anderson connectors. Charged from the car's 13-pin socket while driving and from a **200 W solar panel** on the roof rack (MC4 via the external solar input).
- **Energy Budget (12V):** Maintained in `scripts/calculate_energy.py` — edit the load table there and re-run as components are chosen. Current estimates: ~64 Ah/day total (compressor fridge ~34, router/RPi/lights/pump ~30). Autonomy with 200 Ah + 200 W solar: **~32 days parked in summer sun, ~5 days in shade/shoulder season, ~3.4 days overcast**; one day's consumption is recovered by ~2 h of driving (30 A DC-DC). The comparison in the script also shows why the gas absorption fridge was rejected: without solar even *it* is battery-limited to ~3 days by the base loads, so solar is required either way — and a compressor fridge with solar then beats gas on every axis (works off-level, no flame under the roof tent, no flue, slide-out compatible).
- **Network:** Teltonika 5G industrial router, coaxial LMR-200/400 to Poynting puck antenna. Waterproof N-connectors in a CNC-milled, recessed panel.

## 3. Propane System (RIGHT Side)
- **Separation:** All propane on the right side for complete isolation from the electrical system.
- **Bottle housing:** 6 kg (P6/PK6) in a **caravan-style gas locker box** on the drawbar, flush against the front wall (`cad/gas_box.scad`). Lockable and weatherproof (stone protection all around, not just a front guard), with **low-level vent slots per EN 1949** (propane sinks) and the regulator/hose inside; the fixed pipe exits a floor gland to the right-side rail. Available off the shelf (poly drawbar boxes, Fiamma/Thule class) or CNC-folded in aluminum tread plate. Mounted on bearers **clamped** around the drawbar — the forward beam carries near-peak bending moment, so no holes in it. (The open CNC cradle + stone guard remains as the budget alternative, `cad/gas_bottle_mount.scad`.)
- **Routing:** Fixed, clamped copper or steel pipes under the right frame. Bulkhead fittings at consumers.
- **External Outlets:** Two fixed gas quick connects (GOK/Truma) in CNC-milled protective niches on the **right wall**: one **front-right** (short hose around the front to the slide-out kitchen counter on the front left) and one **rear-right** (griddle/outdoor kitchen at the tail). Mounted low-ish — propane is heavier than air, so the connects stay below the 230V inlet and away from any opening into the cabin — but above wheel-spray height.

## 4. Water System & Interior
- **Water Tank (mounting UNDER REVIEW):** 40-liter **low-profile** tank (550x450x160, food-grade with baffles), modeled in `cad/water_tank.scad` but currently removed from the main assembly — the underslung mounting is being reworked. Candidate spot: **behind the axle, centered** (x≈1400) — freed up now that no spare wheel is carried; there is no drawbar under the floor there (the beam ends at x=1020) and it balances the gas box up front. Caveat: with water behind the axle, the tongue load *rises* as the tank drains (see the dry-tank line in `calculate_mass.py`). Shallow on purpose: at 160 mm depth the skid plate sits ~405 mm above ground, level with the axle tube, instead of being the trailer's lowest point. Protected by a 3 mm aluminum tread skid plate with a beveled leading edge. Mass is kept as a placeholder in the tongue budget.
- **Water Components:** External lockable filler (in Dibond wall), 12V pressure-controlled galley pump (Seaflo/Shurflo), central drain valve.
- **Body:** Floor in 12-15 mm vehicle plywood (formply). Sides in 3-4 mm Dibond on steel/aluminum framework.
- **Galley & Bay Layout (sides in TRAVEL direction, `cad/kitchen_drawer.scad`):**
  - **LEFT, front → rear:** kitchen side-drawer, then storage cabinet.
    1. **Kitchen side-drawer (front left):** 520 x 680 mm drawer on 100-150 kg lock-in/out slides, pulling out *sideways* through an opening in the left wall — worktop over the rear half, open bins in front. Structural slides only in CAD; taps/stove are loose equipment.
    2. **Storage cabinet (mid left):** Fixed plywood cabinet (650 x 450 x 550) for utensils/dry goods, exterior door in the left wall (x 780-1240).
  - **RIGHT, front → rear:** electrical bay, then fridge drawer.
    3. **Electrical bay (mid right):** Mirrored cabinet housing the removable **power-station box** and distribution; the el-niche (router/connection panel) sits in its front bay, and the exterior door in the right wall (x 780-1240) lets the box lift out. Directly ahead of the fridge = short, fixed 12V run to the trailer's biggest consumer.
    4. **Fridge drawer (rear right):** A fridge-sized tray (640 x 450, low sides) on slides, pulling out *rearward* through a **fridge-sized hatch on the rear right** (500 x 520 mm, sill-free at floor level) — the rest of the rear wall, including the whole rear left, is solid. Holds the **12V compressor fridge** (e.g., Dometic CFX or Engel). A compressor fridge works off-level, has no flame/flue, and integrates with the CAN-controlled fridge relay — a 3-way absorption fridge was rejected: it must be near-level to run, needs a fixed flue and ventilation on gas (EN 1949), and an open-flame appliance on a moving drawer would be very hard to get approved.

  **The energy chains to both drawers carry electrical only** — propane stays on fixed pipes to external quick-connects. CNC-milled ventilation grille in the trailer side for the fridge compressor. Note: **no spare wheel is carried on the trailer** — the wheel spec matches the Ford Ranger exactly (6x139.7, 265/60R18 on original rims), so the car's underslung spare serves both. That is the shared-spare design goal paying off, and it keeps the tailgate free for the fridge drawer.

## 5. Road Legal & Registration (Sweden)
- **Approval:** An amateur-built trailer requires *registreringsbesiktning* (individual approval) before road use. Contact the inspection body (Besikta/Opus/Dekra) early — their requirements drive design decisions.
- **Weight class (DECIDED):** The trailer will be **braked** (overrun/auflauf brakes — braked torsion axles with 6x139.7 hubs exist from Knott/AL-KO). This permits registering a total weight above 750 kg, giving real payload margin — the current curb-weight estimate (~585 kg) leaves almost nothing under an unbraked 750 kg rating. The running weight and tongue-load budget is maintained in `scripts/calculate_mass.py`.
- **Tongue load:** Target roughly 5-10% of total weight; up to ~12% is acceptable (and stability-positive) as long as it stays within the coupling's S-value and the Ford Ranger towbar's rated ball load. `calculate_mass.py` reports both the water-full and water-empty states — with the tank behind the axle, the tongue rises as water is consumed.
- **Lighting & marking:** Full lighting per UNECE R48 (tail/brake/indicators, number-plate lamp, rear fog, reversing light where required), triangular rear reflectors (trailer-specific), side/front reflectors. E-marked components only.
- **Mudguards/fenders:** Required — not yet in the CAD model.
- **Coupling & safety:** Type-approved coupling head matched to the Ranger's ball height, plus breakaway cable (or safety chain for unbraked class) and jockey wheel.
