# Concept: Gas & Water Systems

## Propane System
- **Safety Principle (UNDER REVIEW):** The original rule was full left/right gas-electric separation. Since the electrical bay and fridge moved to the RIGHT side (2026-07 layout change), this principle is *parked* — the layout is being fixed first, then the gas routing and quick-connect positions will be refined around it (options: route gas along the left rail instead, or keep right-side pipes with vertical/distance separation from the electrical bay).
- **Front Placement:** The P6/PK6 bottle stands on the drawbar in a CNC-milled cradle, pushed back **flush against the front wall** (~45 mm gap) — tucked into the wall's wind shadow rather than hanging exposed halfway out the drawbar. A bent tread plate (stone guard) wraps the front of the bottle; heavy-duty straps cross-lash it to the cradle.
- **External Outlets:** Two CNC-milled niches in the trailer's right Dibond side: **front-right** (short hose around the front corner feeds a gas stove on the slide-out kitchen counter, front left) and **rear-right** (Muurikka griddle or loose outdoor kitchen at the tail). The niches ensure that the quick connects (GOK/Truma with valve) do not protrude and get knocked off during tight forest driving. Both sit low-ish — propane sinks, so they stay below the 230V inlet (front wall, right half, high) and away from cabin openings — but above wheel-spray height.
- **No Gas to the Drawers:** Propane terminates at the fixed external quick-connects — nothing flexible, and nothing routed alongside electrical cables. The rear fridge drawer holds a 12V compressor unit (works off-level, no flame, no flue — an absorption fridge would need to be level and vented per EN 1949, which is incompatible with a slide-out), and the stove connects outdoors to the front-right quick-connect. The energy chains to both drawers carry **electrical only**, which keeps the left/right gas-electric separation intact all the way.

## Water System (Centered)
- **Center of Gravity:** The 40L tank weighs nearly 45 kg full. Placed just ahead of the torsion-axle tube so consumption doesn't drastically swing the tongue weight, and **offset LEFT of the central drawbar** — the beam runs under the frame centerline (y 575-625), so a centered tank is geometrically impossible. The left offset usefully counterbalances the right-side galley mass (battery box + fridge).
- **Protection:** Suspended in a steel frame/straps under the trailer. Protected from below with a heavy-duty 3 mm aluminum tread plate (skid plate) with a 45-degree beveled leading edge. **Low-profile tank (160 mm deep) on purpose:** a 220 mm tank hung to ~336 mm ground clearance and was the trailer's lowest point; at 160 mm the skid sits at ~405 mm, roughly level with the axle tube, so wheels/axle meet obstacles first and the skid only takes glancing hits. (Alternative if this still feels exposed: move the tank inside the cabin above the floor — full protection and frost-friendlier, at the cost of cargo space and higher CG.)
- **Pipe Routing:** A 12V galley pump is mounted indoors (frost-free/protected) on rubber mounts to absorb pump noise. Externally lockable filler cap in the Dibond wall.
- **Drainage:** An absolute requirement is a low point with a drain valve that can be opened from the side of the trailer before freezing temperatures.
